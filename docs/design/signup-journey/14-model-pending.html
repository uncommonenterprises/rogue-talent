Design notes
  • This is the model's dashboard/profile home while Pending. The amber rt-status badge appears both in the top nav (compact) and large on the card, per the badge spec's placement guidance.
  • The "verify now" callout is the one cobalt action on this page: there's genuinely nothing else actionable to do while waiting, so it's safe to make it primary rather than secondary.
  • No invented review-time estimate ("usually 2 days" etc.). We don't have real data yet to back a number.
  • Revision 3 (Neil, 29/09): replaced the old info-list (one tick plus two empty circles, which read like an unticked checklist) with a vertical progress tracker showing all four steps of the journey: 1 Submitted (done), 2 Review by our team (current, labelled "In progress" in amber), 3 Verify with Stripe (to do - a muted numbered circle, not an empty one), 4 Live in search (to do). Done, current and to-do are distinguished by fill and border colour as well as the explicit status label, so the state is clear at a glance rather than reading as a checklist someone forgot to finish.
  • Revision 3 (Neil, 29/09): the "not visible in search until approved and verified" message is now one short plain sentence under the tracker, rather than a list item, since it's the one thing worth saying once it's clear the model still has steps to go.
  • Revision 3 (Neil, 29/09): the alternate "already verified while waiting" state now shows the tracker itself, with step 3 (Verify with Stripe) marked done while step 2 (Review by our team) is still current/in progress - this replaces the old green subnote, which repeated information the tracker now shows directly.
  • RT-FB-10 (Neil, 28/09): "availability" is not part of the submitted checklist (there's no onboarding availability step any more), and a quiet "Manage your calendar" link is added, deliberately understated (a plain text link, not a button) since it's entirely optional. Copy is explicit that models are available by default and still confirm every booking request themselves, so this never reads as something they forgot to do.
