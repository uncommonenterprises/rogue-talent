Design notes
  • Decline is a page, not a pop-up. Back works, the message preview has room beside the form on desktop, and on a phone it sits between the form and the button so the reviewer reads before committing. The preview is the confirmation: it shows the actual subject and body, filled in live as the reviewer picks and types, which is why there is no separate "Are you sure?" step. The button is disabled until a reason is picked (and a note, for "not suitable"). Try it: pick a reason, or press "Fill an example".
  • The one cobalt action on the decline page is "Decline profile". It is the page's single purpose, so it takes the primary slot; Cancel is secondary. The label is explicit and the preview sits right beside it. It is not red, for the same reason as on R02: this is a routine outcome that the person can fix.
  • A fixed list, plus a free note. The list gives every decline a clear, consistent, kind sentence even when the reviewer is in a hurry; the note adds the specific detail. The note is shown to the user word for word in quotes, so the hint says so. Reviewer names are never shown to the user.
  • Honest about timing. The decision is saved at once; the email goes out from a background job within about 10 minutes. So the page says "Will be sent shortly", never "sent", and changes to "Sent at 14:16" only if the job records that. If it cannot, the page keeps saying "will be sent shortly" and never claims more than it knows (see the flags on the index page).
  • After a decision there are no Approve or Decline buttons. The panel shows who decided, when, the checklist result for an approval, the reason and note for a decline, and the email state. A reviewer who opens a stale email link sees the decision, not a second chance to act. One cobalt action remains: "Review next", which takes the oldest waiting person and is how a reviewer clears the queue in one sitting.
  • Approve gets a short dialog (bottom sheet on phones) because approving makes someone visible to clients and cannot be quietly undone. It states exactly what will happen next, which depends on the Stripe status: nothing is promised that is not true.
  • Reversing a decision is deliberately not on this page. See open question 2.
  • Reason wording follows the existing "Not approved" copy on the account status page ("wasn't approved this time", "not a permanent decision"). It is draft and is public-facing, so it needs Neil's approval.
