Design notes
  • Decline is a page, not a pop-up. Back works, the message preview has room beside the form on desktop, and on a phone it sits between the form and the button so the reviewer reads before committing. The preview is the confirmation: it shows the actual subject and body, filled in live as the reviewer picks and types, which is why there is no separate "Are you sure?" step. The button is disabled until a reason is picked (and a note, for "not suitable"). Try it: pick a reason, or press "Fill an example".
  • The one cobalt action on the decline page is "Decline profile". It is the page's single purpose, so it takes the primary slot; Cancel is secondary. The label is explicit and the preview sits right beside it. It is not red, for the same reason as on R02: this is a routine outcome that the person can fix.
  • A fixed list, plus a free note. The list gives every decline a clear, consistent, kind sentence even when the reviewer is in a hurry; the note adds the specific detail. The note is shown to the user word for word in quotes, so the hint says so. Reviewer names are never shown to the user.
  • Decline wording is approved (Neil, 07/11/2026). Five model reasons and five client reasons, as written. "Contact details or social handles" was removed from the model list because models keep their website and Instagram fields. The DRAFT flags on the decline list are gone.
  • Flag a safety concern is a page, then a dialog, then a state. Reason (four options) and a required note; a confirmation that restates the four effects; then "Flagged - with the safety team". Neither cobalt nor red: Continue and Flag concern are ink-keyline secondary buttons, so the one cobalt action on R02 and R04 stays Approve, and red stays for errors. These views have no cobalt button by design. The reason list and the guidance wording are marked DRAFT because they go to the solicitor.
  • Flag sends nothing to the person. No email, and their status page keeps saying it is under review. The alert goes to safety@ (the existing safeguarding alert). The note is for the safety team only. The case stays open until Neil or a named safety lead decides; the screen where they record that outcome is not part of this set (see the flags for the PM on the index page).
  • Flag is available after approval, too. On the decided page it sits below the buttons, behind a hairline, and hides the profile again straight away. It is not offered after a decline, because a declined profile is already hidden. Reviewers and admins both see it.
  • "Reopen review" is admin only. It sits in its own "Admin only" block at the bottom of the decided page. It hides the profile again immediately, puts the person back in the queue and sends no email. Reviewers never see it, and nothing on their page hints it exists. The confirm dialog is a small design addition, because it hides a live profile; drop it if Neil prefers a single click.
  • Honest about timing. The decision is saved at once; the email goes out from a background job within about 10 minutes. So the page says "Will be sent shortly", never "sent", and changes to "Sent at 14:16" only if the job records that. If it cannot, the page keeps saying "will be sent shortly" and never claims more than it knows (see the flags on the index page).
  • After a decision there are no Approve or Decline buttons. The panel shows who decided, when, the checklist result for an approval, the reason and note for a decline, and the email state. A reviewer who opens a stale email link sees the decision, not a second chance to act. One cobalt action remains: "Review next", which takes the oldest waiting person and is how a reviewer clears the queue in one sitting.
  • Approve gets a short dialog (bottom sheet on phones) because approving makes someone visible to clients. It states exactly what will happen next, which depends on the Stripe status: nothing is promised that is not true.
  • Reversing a decision (decided 07/11/2026): there is no undo on a decision for reviewers. Admins can reopen a review, and anyone can flag a safety concern after approval.
  • Reason wording follows the existing "Not approved" copy on the account status page ("wasn't approved this time", "not a permanent decision").
