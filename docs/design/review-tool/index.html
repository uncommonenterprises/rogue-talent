Approve or decline someone in one tap from the email.

An in-site tool for reviewers to approve or decline models and clients, replacing hand-edited metadata in Console. The reviewer clicks the link in the email, lands on the person in Rogue Talent, ticks a short checklist and decides. Sharetribe changes happen in the background. Built on the same tokens and rt-* components as the booking set. Nothing here is live; it is for approval before the build. All names, numbers, addresses and photos are example data, and every picture is a labelled placeholder.

Email, log in if needed, look, decide. Reviewers may well do this on a phone, so R01 and R02 are shown at 375px inside each page.

Key design decisions

One cobalt action: Approve

Approve is the only cobalt button on a review page. Decline sits directly under it as a secondary (ink keyline) button. On the decline page, "Decline profile" is the single primary because it is that page's one job.

Decline is not red

Red is for danger. A decline is a routine outcome the person can fix and resubmit, with no limit, so it is neutral grey in the queue and secondary on the page. Red appears only when something fails (a decision not saved).

The checklist gates Approve

Approve stays disabled until all five boxes are ticked, with a plain line saying why. Decline never needs ticks. This makes the checklist a real check, not decoration (open question 1).

The preview is the confirmation

Decline shows the actual email subject and body, filled in live. No extra "are you sure" step. Approve gets a short dialog that says what will happen, which depends on the Stripe status.

Honest about the email

The email goes from a background job in about 10 minutes, so every screen says "will be sent shortly", never "sent". It changes to "Sent at 14:16" only if the job records it.

Calm and functional

No marketing voice. Hairlines, the existing status chips, no new colours, no gradients, no emoji. Amber marks only slow waits and draft flags. Photos are placeholders.

Phone first for R01 and R02

Rows become two-line cards; the profile page puts a summary card first and the checklist and buttons at the end, with a bottom bar that jumps to them. 44px minimum tap targets, nothing scrolls sideways.

Only what the decision needs

Clients show company, number, role and ID status. No email, phone or date of birth on screen. Fewer personal details is the safer default for people who are not Neil.

Open questions for Neil

1. Should ticking the whole checklist be required before Approve works?

As designed, Approve is disabled until all five boxes are ticked. It stops a rushed approve and gives you something to point to if a bad profile gets through. The cost is a few extra taps on every approval. Decline never needs ticks.

Recommendation: yes, required. Five taps is a small price for a safety-facing gate, and it is what makes "reviewed by our team" true in practice. Relax it later if the wording proves too fussy.

2. Can a decision be reversed from the page?

As designed there is no undo. After a decision the page shows who decided and when, and no buttons. A mistaken decline can be fixed by the person resubmitting (no limit on attempts). A mistaken approve would be fixed in Console by suspending the account.

Recommendation: no undo in v1. The Approve dialog and the decline preview are the safeguards. Add an admin-only "Reopen review" button only if mistakes actually happen.

3. Are the decline reasons and their wording right? (public-facing)

The six model reasons and five client reasons on R03 each send a fixed sentence, plus the reviewer's optional note word for word in quotes, to a real person by email and on their account status page. The reviewer's name is never shown to them. The tone follows the existing "wasn't approved this time" copy.

Recommendation: approve the list as a draft and read the sentences yourself, since they speak for the company. Keep the note visible in the preview so reviewers see exactly what goes out.

4. Does every reviewer get every email?

As designed, each new submission emails all reviewers. The first decision wins; anyone opening the link afterwards sees the decision (R07), and the server refuses a second one. The alternative is assigning each submission to one reviewer.

Recommendation: everyone gets everything while there are three reviewers or fewer. It is simpler to build and nobody becomes a bottleneck. Move to assignment if you grow past that.

5. Build the Team page now, or add reviewers in Console for launch?

R06 lets an admin add or remove reviewers by email. The developer is checking how hard that is without Console. The review screens do not depend on it.

Recommendation: if it is more than a small job, add the first few reviewers in Console and build R06 later. It will be used rarely, and a handful of reviewers is a one-minute Console job each.

Flags for the PM (not questions for Neil)

  • Links field versus "no social handles". The B03 profile shows a Links field (website, Instagram). If handles are banned from "photos or text", that field contradicts checklist item 3. A rule is needed: either Links is exempt (and the checklist says so) or the field changes.
  • Under-18 rule. The checklist asks "appears to be over 18", but the decline list has no under-18 reason, on purpose (we should not tell a possible minor why). What a reviewer does when this box cannot be ticked needs an agreed procedure. The lifecycle spec says an under-18 account stays blocked for operator review. Safety surface.
  • "Role or use case" for clients is in the brief but is not a field in the business-details form (signup screen 18). Add it to the form or drop the row on R04.
  • Email sent state. "Sent at 14:16" needs the background job to record a timestamp the page can read. If it cannot, the page keeps saying "will be sent shortly" and the queue's email column shows only that.
  • Public claim now backed by the tool. B03 says "Profile and portfolio reviewed by the Rogue Talent team". This tool, with the checklist ticks recorded against each decision, is what makes that sentence evidence-based. Recommend storing who decided, when, the checklist result and the reason with every decision.
  • Reviewer accounts. They are ordinary Rogue Talent accounts with a reviewer flag. Which user type they hold, and where the log-in redirect sends them (it must return to the review page), are Developer questions.
  • Where the decline reason lives. The account status page currently shows one quoted note ("What our reviewer noted"). This design sends a fixed sentence plus an optional note, so that page must show both. Store the reason code and the note separately.
  • Shared styles. review.css imports ../booking-journey/journey.css instead of copying it a third time. Keep both folders together, or copy the file if this folder is moved.

What the Developer needs to know

  • Decision and gating: Approve and Decline write the existing operator decision (reviewDecision and the private note) per the account status lifecycle amendment. This is the safety-critical gate; human review before real users.
  • First decision wins: the server must refuse a second decision on someone already decided, and the page must show the R07 message.
  • Return to page after log-in: a reviewer who clicks the email link while logged out must land on the review page after logging in.
  • No access page: identical for a real review URL and a made-up one, for anyone who is not a reviewer.
  • Companies House link: built from the company number as https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/search/companies?q={number}, opening in a new tab. Please confirm the URL still works when you build it. No automatic lookup in v1.
  • Reviewer emails: one per submission to every reviewer, with the three subjects shown on R05. Sent from the same transport as the other lifecycle emails.
  • Profile reuse: R02 is the B03 profile with the booking panel replaced by the review panel and the reviews and "Checked before going live" sections removed.
  • Components used: existing rt-btn, rt-field, rt-check, rt-tag, rt-status, rt-avatar, rt-badge. New and small: rt-tab with a count, the queue row, the review panel, the message preview card, and the dialog. No new colours, fonts, spacing or radii.

Escalations (not mine to decide)

  • Public-facing words: the decline reasons and their sentences (question 3), which reach real people.
  • Safety surfaces: the age check procedure and the checklist wording, which is marked DRAFT on every panel.
  • Privacy and legal: reviewers other than Neil see identity-adjacent details (photos, ID verification status, company details). Who gets access, and what they may see, should be confirmed before real users.
  • Product rules: questions 1, 2 and 4 each set a rule for how reviewing works.
