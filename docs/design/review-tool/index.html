Mock Rogue Talent - review tool 7 screens · click any card to open
Design review · ux-designer → PM → Neil · updated 07/11/2026

Approve or decline someone in one tap from the email.

An in-site tool for reviewers to approve or decline models and clients, replacing hand-edited metadata in Console. The reviewer clicks the link in the email, lands on the person in Rogue Talent, ticks a short checklist and decides. Sharetribe changes happen in the background. Built on the same tokens and rt-* components as the booking set. Nothing here is live. All names, numbers, addresses and photos are example data, and every picture is a labelled placeholder. This version includes Neil's decisions of 07/11/2026.

What a reviewer does

Email, log in if needed, look, decide. Reviewers may well do this on a phone, so R01 and R02 are shown at 375px inside each page.

Decisions (Neil, 07/11/2026)

  • Flag a safety concern is a third action on the model and client review pages (R02, R04). The profile stays hidden and cannot be approved or booked. No email goes to the person, and their status page keeps saying it is under review. An immediate alert goes to safety@ (the existing safeguarding alert). The case stays open until Neil or a named safety lead decides. It covers suspected under-18, stolen photos and signs of coercion. It has a short form (reason, plus a required note), a confirmation step and a "Flagged - with the safety team" state, and it is also available after approval, where it hides the profile again. It is a quiet text action, apart from Approve and Decline, neither cobalt nor red. The reason list and guidance are DRAFT, going to the solicitor.
  • Admin-only "Reopen review" on the decided page. It hides the profile again immediately, puts the person back in the queue and sends no email. Reviewers never see it.
  • Checklist item 3 is now "No nudity in photos". Models keep their website and Instagram fields. The checklist stays at five items, all required before Approve.
  • Decline reasons: "Contact details or social handles" is removed for models. All other decline wording is approved as written.
  • Queue amber: anything waiting more than 1 working day (weekends excluded) shows amber. No red.
  • Reviewer emails: every reviewer gets every submission email. The first decision wins.
  • Team page (R06): decided for build stage 3. Until then the PM adds reviewers in Console.
  • Resolved flags: the Links flag (links stay, checklist changed); the under-18 flag (handled through "Flag a safety concern"); "role or use case" for clients is dropped, because it is not collected.

Key design decisions

One cobalt action: Approve

Approve is the only cobalt button on a review page. Decline sits directly under it as a secondary (ink keyline) button. On the decline page, "Decline profile" is the single primary because it is that page's one job.

Decline is not red

Red is for danger. A decline is a routine outcome the person can fix and resubmit, with no limit, so it is neutral grey in the queue and secondary on the page. Red appears only when something fails (a decision not saved).

Flag is quiet, not loud

"Flag a safety concern" is an underlined text action below a hairline, away from Approve and Decline so it is not clicked by accident. It is neither cobalt nor red. It acts at once and says exactly what happens, including that the person is not emailed.

The checklist gates Approve

Approve stays disabled until all five boxes are ticked, with a plain line saying why. Decline and Flag never need ticks. This makes the checklist a real check, not decoration.

The preview is the confirmation

Decline shows the actual email subject and body, filled in live. No extra "are you sure" step. Approve and Flag each get a short dialog that says what will happen.

Honest about the email

The email goes from a background job in about 10 minutes, so every screen says "will be sent shortly", never "sent". It changes to "Sent at 14:16" only if the job records it. A flag sends no email, and says so.

Calm and functional

No marketing voice. Hairlines, the existing status chips, no new colours, no gradients, no emoji. Amber marks only slow waits (more than 1 working day) and draft flags. Photos are placeholders.

Phone first for R01 and R02

Rows become two-line cards; the profile page puts a summary card first and the checklist and buttons at the end, with a bottom bar that jumps to them. 44px minimum tap targets, nothing scrolls sideways.

Only what the decision needs

Clients show company, number, contact name and ID status. No email, phone or date of birth on screen. Fewer personal details is the safer default for people who are not Neil.

Flags for the PM (not questions for Neil)

  • Safety outcome screen. The brief says the flagged case stays open until Neil or a named safety lead decides. This set shows the flag and the flagged state, not the screen where that person records the outcome (clear and restore, or close the account). Needs scoping with the safety framework.
  • Flag on client accounts. R04 uses the same four reasons. "Photos may not be theirs" will rarely apply to a client. Worth the solicitor's view when the list goes to them.
  • Client checklist after dropping "role or use case". The checklist item that relied on it was replaced so the list stays at five: "Industry and website look like a business that would book models" (companies) and "The business looks like one that would book models" (sole traders). Checklist wording is still draft.
  • Working days in the queue. The amber rule counts working days, weekends excluded. Bank holidays are not mentioned in the decision; the build needs a rule (suggest: weekends only for v1).
  • Reopen review confirmation. I added a short confirm dialog because it hides a live profile. It is easy to drop if Neil wants a single click.
  • Email sent state. "Sent at 14:16" needs the background job to record a timestamp the page can read. If it cannot, the page keeps saying "will be sent shortly" and the queue's email column shows only that.
  • Public claim now backed by the tool. B03 says "Profile and portfolio reviewed by the Rogue Talent team". This tool, with the checklist ticks recorded against each decision, is what makes that sentence evidence-based. Recommend storing who decided, when, the checklist result and the reason with every decision, and who flagged, when, why and the note with every flag.
  • Reviewer accounts. They are ordinary Rogue Talent accounts with a reviewer flag. Which user type they hold, and where the log-in redirect sends them (it must return to the review page), are Developer questions.
  • Where the decline reason lives. The account status page currently shows one quoted note ("What our reviewer noted"). This design sends a fixed sentence plus an optional note, so that page must show both. Store the reason code and the note separately. A flagged person's status page must keep showing "under review", with no hint of the flag.
  • Shared styles. review.css imports ../booking-journey/journey.css instead of copying it a third time. Keep both folders together, or copy the file if this folder is moved.

What the Developer needs to know

  • Decision and gating: Approve and Decline write the existing operator decision (reviewDecision and the private note) per the account status lifecycle amendment. This is the safety-critical gate; human review before real users.
  • First decision wins: the server must refuse a second decision on someone already decided, and the page must show the R07 message.
  • Flag a safety concern: keeps the profile hidden, sends no email to the person, fires the existing safeguarding alert to safety@ immediately, and leaves the case open. Allowed before and after approval (after approval it hides the profile again). The person's status page must not change.
  • Reopen review: admin only, enforced on the server, not just hidden in the page. Hides the profile immediately, returns the person to the queue, sends no email.
  • Queue amber: more than 1 working day since submission, weekends excluded.
  • Return to page after log-in: a reviewer who clicks the email link while logged out must land on the review page after logging in.
  • No access page: identical for a real review URL and a made-up one, for anyone who is not a reviewer.
  • Companies House link: built from the company number as https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/search/companies?q={number}, opening in a new tab. Please confirm the URL still works when you build it. No automatic lookup in v1.
  • Reviewer emails: one per submission to every reviewer (decided), with the three subjects shown on R05. Sent from the same transport as the other lifecycle emails.
  • Profile reuse: R02 is the B03 profile with the booking panel replaced by the review panel and the reviews and "Checked before going live" sections removed.
  • Components used: existing rt-btn, rt-field, rt-check, rt-tag, rt-status, rt-avatar, rt-badge. New and small: rt-tab with a count, the queue row, the review panel, the message preview card, the dialog, and the quiet flag link (rv-flag). No new colours, fonts, spacing or radii.

Escalations (not mine to decide)

  • Safety surfaces, still DRAFT: the checklist wording, and the safety-concern reason list and guidance (going to the solicitor).
  • Privacy and legal: reviewers other than Neil see identity-adjacent details (photos, ID verification status, company details). Who gets access, and what they may see, should be confirmed before real users.
