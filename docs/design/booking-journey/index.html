From first search to a confirmed booking, on desktop and phone.

Every screen a client sees from "find a model" to "request sent", and the model's side of the same request: accept or decline, and the status both of them read afterwards. Built on the same tokens and rt-* components as the approved sign-up set. Nothing here is live; it is for review before any of it goes to the Developer. 12. Should dates inside the model's minimum notice be unavailable in the profile calendar?

Decided 07/11/2026: yes, grey them out (Neil: "Yes, grey them out").

The brief asks for it, and it keeps the calendar honest at a glance. But open question 3 says notice is a guide: B03 and B04 let a client request inside it and the model can decline. With an inline calendar that picks dates, muting them quietly enforces notice, and a client with an urgent shoot has no way to ask. It is a product rule, so it is yours.

Recommendation: mute them, as designed. Models set a notice precisely so late requests do not arrive, and a calendar that shows a date as bookable and then warns is worse. Question 3 then becomes "enforced in the picker, still decided by the model". If urgent shoots matter, add a "message about short notice" route later.

13. Who should see a model's free dates?

Decided 08/11/2026: logged-in, approved clients only (Neil: "Yes, approved clients only").

Showing which days a model is free tells any visitor, logged in or not, when she is likely to be busy or alone. That is a privacy and safety surface, and it is also easy to scrape.

Recommendation: show the calendar only to logged-in, approved clients. Everyone else sees "Log in to see Imogen's free dates" in the same place (state mocked). Costs a little discovery for logged-out visitors, which the profile still serves with rates and portfolio.

14. Should a pending request make a date look unavailable?

Decided 08/11/2026: a date held by a waiting request shows exactly like a booked date, plain "not available" (Neil: "Yes, plain not available"). No separate "requested" state.

Today only accepted bookings and the model's own blocks remove a date. A date can show green while another client's request for it is waiting for the model, so two clients can ask for the same day and one will be declined or expire. The note says the model confirms every request, so it is honest, but it can still disappoint.

Correction (PM, 08/11/2026): the premise above is wrong. In Sharetribe a pending booking reserves the date (Integration API booking states: pending "reserves time slot: yes"). A request holds the date for at most 48 hours (or until the shoot starts), then it is released if not accepted. Two clients cannot ask for the same day.

Recommendation: leave pending requests alone for v1, keep the note, and watch how often it happens. Dimming dates on pending requests would also tell visitors how busy a model is, and a single request could hide a date from everyone for 48 hours.

Why nine screens, and what moved

  • Filters (B02) is its own file because on a phone it is a different surface (a full-height sheet) with its own states, and on desktop the same panel is the sidebar. B01 contains the live panel too.
  • Date selection (B04) and Price breakdown (B05) are components shown standalone rather than only inside the profile and checkout. Each is used in four places, so a single reference stops them drifting apart. They are also embedded and live in B03 and B06.
  • The booking panel and its states are inside B03, not a separate screen, because on desktop it is part of the page and on phones it is the bar and sheet.
  • Request sent (B07) is the "just sent" top card of the booking page; B09 shows what that same page says in every other state.
  • City and dates moved out of the filter panel into the search header on all sizes, so the two most important criteria are always visible.

Design principles applied

One cobalt action per view

Select dates or Request to book; Send booking request; Accept request; Go to your bookings. Chips, selected days and numbered steps are ink, never cobalt. (The panel-states strip on B03 shows several panels side by side for reference; each is its own view in product.)

Say what happens to the money

Every price screen states whether the card is charged. Requested, Declined and Expired say "not charged". The model sees 100% of their rate.

Safety without theatre

Private residence is a visible choice with a calm note, the model's boundary is never revealed, and the shareable summary says plainly that Rogue Talent does not monitor shoots.

Only real, enforced facts

The 48-hour deadline, the 48-hour cancellation cutoff and the payout promise are all enforced by the process. Minimum notice is not, so it is shown as a guide. No response-time or rating claims.

Phone first

Sheets instead of pop-overs, 44px tap targets, one column, and a summary that collapses. Designed at 375px; desktop adds columns.

No stock photography, no gendered pronouns

Every picture is the labelled stripe. Copy uses the model's name or "the day rate", never he or she inferred from a field.

Open questions for Neil

1. Is it right to keep the per-card "Verified" badge retired and state the standard once instead?

The lifecycle spec retired the badge because every visible model is Verified, so it signals nothing. B01 states the standard in one line under the heading ("Every model here is reviewed by our team and ID verified"). B03 repeats it as a section, "Checked before going live", at the moment of booking. Both are public safety and verification claims. They are true today, in wording already used on the approved client "ready to book" screen.

Recommendation: approve as designed. It tells new clients the standard without implying some models are unverified. Please confirm the exact wording since it is a public claim.

2. Half-day and hourly rates are collected and shown, but only whole days can be booked. What should the profile do?

B03 shows all three rates and labels half-day and hourly "for reference", with a sentence saying bookings are charged per full day. Showing prices a client cannot select invites "can you do half a day for £260?" and an off-platform deal, which is also a fee leak.

Recommendation: for v1 show them as designed, with the honest note. If clients keep asking, make half-day a bookable option next (a process and price change, not a design one). Hiding them is the other option if you would rather not invite the question.

3. Minimum notice: should the system enforce it?

Today it is a stated preference only (the model can decline any request). B03 and B04 show it as a guide and, if a client picks a start date inside it, a calm non-blocking line. Enforcing it would be a product rule change and would hide dates models may well accept.

Recommendation: keep it a guide for v1, as designed. Revisit if models report requests arriving too late. (See also question 12, which touches this.)

4. Billing details: collect only name, postcode and country?

B06 drops the street address, "Apt #", city and "State" that the template asks for, keeping name on card, billing postcode and country (default United Kingdom). We already hold a client's business address from onboarding. This changes what is sent to Stripe as billing details.

Recommendation: yes. It is how UK card checkouts work and it removes most of the fields behind M6. The Developer should confirm Stripe's postcode checks still behave as you want.

5. When a model charges travel separately, how is it paid?

The profile and the price breakdown say plainly that travel is not in the total and can be agreed in the booking conversation. Nothing collects it through Rogue Talent, so it would be paid outside the platform, and the 15% fee does not apply to it.

Recommendation: accept this for v1 and keep the honest wording. Decide before launch whether to add an optional "travel" line the client pays through the platform; it is the cleaner answer but is new scope.

6. Keep ethnicity as a search filter?

It was a deliberate earlier choice, and docs/discovery-model-fields.md flags it as legally and ethically sensitive. B01 and B02 keep it, but last in the list and collapsed on B01.

Recommendation: keep it as decided, last and collapsed, and ask the solicitor to look at it in the same review as the content licence. Removing it is a one-line Console change if they advise it.

7. Remove the "Contact" button before a request is sent?

The template's author card has a Contact button. B03 removes it with the rest of "About the listing author". Clients can still put questions in the request note, and message the model once a request exists (B07). That keeps unsolicited messages to models, and conversation outside a booking, to a minimum.

Recommendation: remove it for launch. It supports the safety posture and keeps conversation tied to a booking. Add it back if clients cannot get answers to simple questions.

8. Show the cancellation policy at checkout?

B06 and B09 say a client can cancel free until 48 hours before the shoot and not after. That matches the two-tier process in docs/booking-process-design.md, but docs/cancellation-process-spec.md records that the published FAQ and Terms still describe three tiers. This is a legal and consumer-rights surface.

Recommendation: show it, but only once the Terms are rewritten to the two-tier policy and the process version is live. Until then, remove the line rather than publish a promise the Terms contradict.

9. Should a client be able to withdraw a request before the model replies?

The process has no cancel from "Requested". A client who sends a request by mistake, or finds another model, must wait up to 48 hours. B07 offers no withdraw link for that reason.

Recommendation: add a small "withdraw request" transition (full release of the card hold) before launch. It is fair to clients and frees the model's inbox. It is a process change, so it needs your approval.

10. Is accepting a booking a sufficient way for the model to agree the content licence?

The draft licence says the model accepts by accepting the booking. B08 puts the sentence "By accepting, you agree to the Content Licence & Model Release for this booking" above the Accept button, with a plain-English summary and the full text on the page. It is a perpetual, all-media, single-fee licence, so this is a legal surface and the wording is still draft.

Recommendation: keep the design, and send B08 to the solicitor along with the licence itself. Do not launch with the "DRAFT" wording visible or with this sentence unreviewed.

11. Is it right to show the shoot address to the model before they accept?

B08 shows the full address, and the location type, so the model can decide, especially for a private residence. The address is visible only to the model and the client.

Recommendation: yes. A model cannot make a safe decision without it. The shareable summary already works this way after acceptance.

  • Edge case to specify: what happens to a pending request if its dates are taken by another accepted booking before the model replies (auto-decline, or Accept fails with a clear message)?

  • Withdraw request (question 9) and the cancellation line (question 8) depend on the process and the Terms, not on design.
  • Copy and dashes: 32 lines in en.json contain an em or en dash. On these booking surfaces they include the fee note (OrderBreakdown.commissionFeeNote), the licence draft notice and terms body, the private-residence safety intro, four shareable-summary strings, three provider cancelled and completed strings, and the "in review" profile banner. Replacements for the fee note, the safety intro and the summary intro are in B05, B06 and B08; the rest need the same treatment before launch.

Escalations (not mine to decide)

  • Public claims: the trust line and the "Checked before going live" section (question 1); "free to cancel until 48 hours before" (question 8).
  • Legal surfaces: the licence summary shown to the client (B06), the sentence above Accept (B08, question 10), and the draft wording flag.
  • Safety surfaces: the private-residence note to the client (B06), the disabled sole-trader state, the model's safety note and shareable summary (B08); who can see a model's free dates (B03b, question 13).
  • Product rules: questions 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9, 12 and 14 each touch a rule, so none is assumed in the design beyond what is stated.
