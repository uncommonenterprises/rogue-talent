# Model Contract Benchmark — Rogue Talent Content Licence vs. Market Norms

> Research pack for the solicitor review of `docs/legal/rogue-talent-content-licence-DRAFT.md`.
> Purpose: benchmark our **single, standard, broad + perpetual, single-fee** "Content Licence & Model
> Release" against real, public modelling-industry contracts and image-usage practice.
> Compiled 2026-09-21 from public web sources (URLs below). Not legal advice.
>
> **Copyright note:** no source's contract text is reproduced in full here — each is summarised in our
> own words with short quoted phrases (<15 words) for attribution only.

---

## TL;DR

- **Two worlds exist.** (1) **Model-agency booking terms** (UK fashion norm) license usage **narrowly and
  for a limited time** (typically **UK-only, one image, one medium, one year / one season**), with
  **extra fees** for every extension of media, territory, duration, or channel. (2) **Photographer↔model
  release forms** (US-rooted, e.g. ASMP-style) grant **broad, perpetual, irrevocable** rights in a
  **single signature** — much closer to our draft.
- **Our draft sits firmly in the release-form tradition, pushed to its most client-favourable end**:
  worldwide + perpetual + all-media + single fee. That is **genuinely disruptive versus UK agency
  practice**, where "usage" is the agency's core recurring revenue line and is deliberately metered.
- **"Perpetual + single fee" is unusual for agency bookings but normal for buyout/release deals.** The
  agency world calls the perpetual-all-media version a **"full buyout"** and prices it at a large premium
  ("triple or quadruple talent fees"). We are giving that buyout away inside the base booking fee — a
  real selling point, but it means the model's protections in the contract must do more work, because the
  usual protection (rights expire, so misuse is time-boxed and re-clearing pays the model again) is gone.
- **Biggest gaps to close before launch** (detail in §5): **AI training / synthetic-likeness** restriction,
  a **category/competitor-conflict** position, a **credit / portfolio** clause, **takedown / breach**
  mechanics, an explicit **image-approval or "no unapproved use of outtakes"** stance, and confirming the
  **moral-rights** mechanism under UK law (CDPA 1988 — waiver vs "consent not to assert").

## 2. The core market split (context for the table)

**Agency booking model (BFMA, Industry, CM, Showcast, Starboxx):** the model provides a service *and* a
**metered licence**. Default grant is deliberately small — Industry/BFMA start at **"one image via a single
published medium"**, **"United Kingdom only"**, for **one year or one season**. Every extension (more media,
social channels, more countries, longer time, exclusivity) is a **separately priced additional fee**, and
rights **"not expressly granted … are reserved to the Agency and/or the model."** The agency actively
**"monitors these image rights and takes care of the payment of the fees earned"** (CM Models) — recurring
usage revenue is the point.

**Release model (ASMP, StudioBinder):** a single form grants the photographer **broad, perpetual, irrevocable**
commercial rights up front, usually for a **single fee** (or none, for TFP), with **sensitive-use carve-outs**.
This is the tradition our draft descends from.

**Why usage is normally time-limited (sanity-check on the disruption thesis):** A Photo Editor is explicit —
**"You are RENTING, not buying an image unless explicitly stated"** — and time limits exist to **protect the
rightsholder's future income** (re-licensing / renewal fees) and to price exposure ("the more exposure, the
higher the price"). In the model world the same logic transfers to the *model's* likeness: perpetual all-media
use is the **"full buyout"**, which agencies price at a steep premium or refuse. So **single-fee perpetual is
demonstrably a real concession to the buyer** — that is exactly why it reads as disruption, and exactly why a
model's lawyer will look hard at what the model gets in return.

## 4. Findings — where our draft matches, leads, and lags

**Where we match market norms**
- **Non-exclusivity** (cl. 8) matches every source's default — models keep working elsewhere. Good.
- **No third-party re-sale/transfer** (cl. 2A) matches the agency norm ("cannot licence the image to a third
  party"). Our "service providers on the Client's behalf" carve-out is sensible and mirrors how agencies let a
  client's printer/agency deliver the client's *own* use — but see §5 on wording it tightly.
- **Sensitive-use carve-outs** (cl. 5) are directionally aligned with both BFMA (no porn/derogatory; written
  approval for sensitive) and ASMP/StudioBinder (sex/politics/religion opt-outs).
- **Worldwide + all-media + perpetual** is a coherent, recognised *shape* — it is the **"full buyout"** the
  agency world already has a name and a price for. We are not inventing a new right, we are **bundling the
  premium right into the base fee**.

**Where we are deliberately more client-favourable (the intended disruption)**
- **Single fee covers perpetual, worldwide, all-media, incl. paid media** — this collapses the entire agency
  fee ladder (territory fees, media fees, renewal fees, social fees, exclusivity-adjacent premiums) into one
  number. Against BFMA/Industry (UK-only/one-medium/one-year base) this is a **dramatic** expansion of buyer
  rights for the money. This is real, defensible disruption — and worth marketing — **provided** the model
  understands and is fairly paid for granting a buyout, which is the fairness question a model's lawyer will raise.
- **"Later devised" media + explicit paid/performance media** puts us at the most buyer-favourable end even
  versus buyout practice, which usually still enumerates channels.
- **Platform-not-a-party** (cl. 9) is cleaner/lighter than the agency model, where the agency is an active
  principal policing usage. Fine for a marketplace, but it means **no one is monitoring misuse for the model** —
  the contract's own remedies must be adequate (see §5 takedown/breach).

**Where we may be missing standard model protections (flag before launch) — see §5.**

## 5. Gaps / risks a model (or their lawyer) would expect — and fixes

Ordered by how strongly the market/precedent supports adding them.

1. **AI training & synthetic likeness — MISSING (highest priority).** The current UK guidance is to
   **"restrict AI training and synthetic reproduction by default; allow only by specific written addendum"**
   (Contractable). A perpetual, all-media, "later devised" licence that is *silent* on AI reads today as
   granting AI/deepfake training rights — a red flag for any model in 2026. **Fix:** add an explicit carve-out
   that the licence does **not** permit using the Content to train generative-AI models or to create synthetic
   likenesses / deepfakes of the Model without separate written consent. This is low-cost and materially
   de-risks the "perpetual" grant.

2. **Credit / portfolio — PARTIALLY MISSING.** BFMA requires model credit ("model's name @ agency"); our
   draft only lets the Model reuse Content in their own portfolio (buried in cl. 2A). **Fix:** promote the
   Model's **portfolio/self-promotion right to its own clause**, and consider an optional (not mandatory)
   credit expectation. Cheap goodwill; matches norm.

3. **Takedown / breach mechanics — MISSING.** Agencies police usage and can force takedowns; our platform-light
   model removes that safety net. If a Client breaches cl. 5 (e.g. defamatory/porn use), the draft gives no
   **remedy, takedown route, or licence-termination-on-breach**. A perpetual irrevocable licence with *no*
   breach exit is unusually harsh on the model. **Fix:** make the licence **revocable on material breach of the
   use-restrictions** (i.e. "irrevocable except on breach of cl. 5"), and add a takedown/notice mechanism (even
   if run via the platform ToS).

4. **Competitor / category conflict — MISSING.** Buyout/exclusivity practice universally lets models avoid
   being the face of directly competing brands; even non-exclusive deals often carry a short conflict window.
   We are non-exclusive (good for the model working) but silent on the *reverse* — a model could unknowingly
   appear for rival brands, or a client could feel the same. **Fix:** decide a position and state it: either
   "no category exclusivity is granted — Client accepts Model may appear for competitors" (keeps it simple and
   is consistent with our non-exclusive stance) **or** offer conflict windows as a paid post-launch toggle.
   Even a one-line acknowledgement pre-empts disputes.

5. **Unapproved use of outtakes / raw content — WORTH CLARIFYING.** "All … material featuring the Model
   created during the booked shoot" (cl. 1) sweeps in every unretouched frame. Models commonly expect a say
   over unflattering outtakes. **Fix:** either confirm this breadth is intended (simplest) or narrow "Content"
   to delivered/selected images, and rely on cl. 5(d) (no misleading alteration) for the rest.

6. **Consideration / fairness recital — WORTH ADDING.** Because the whole disruption rests on "single fee =
   perpetual buyout", the contract is stronger (and fairer-looking) if it **says so on its face**: a short
   recital that the Model **acknowledges the booking fee is agreed as full consideration for a perpetual
   worldwide licence**, and has priced accordingly. StarNow-style model guidance stresses models should be
   "compensated accordingly" for perpetual rights; an explicit acknowledgement both respects that and shores up
   enforceability/"unfair terms" resilience (esp. where the model is a consumer/sole trader).

7. **Moral rights mechanism — CONFIRM (already flagged in draft).** Under UK law (CDPA 1988) moral rights of a
   *performer/subject* are narrow and a blanket "waiver" may need to be framed as a **"consent not to assert"**.
   Draft already asks the solicitor this (good) — keep it on the list; it interacts with cl. 5(d).

8. **Nudity / intimate content approval — CONSIDER.** BFMA and Industry both require **"express prior written
   approval"** for nude/semi-nude imagery. Our draft bans obscene/porn *use* but doesn't address consent to
   *create* intimate content. Likely lives in booking flow / ToS rather than the licence, but confirm it is
   captured somewhere.

## 6. Bottom line for the PM / solicitor

- The **shape** of our licence (broad, worldwide, perpetual, non-exclusive, single-fee, no third-party re-sale)
  is coherent and maps to a recognised industry construct — the **"full buyout"** — so it is defensible, not
  eccentric. What is disruptive is **bundling that buyout into one flat booking fee with no usage ladder**,
  which is a genuine, marketable departure from UK agency norms (BFMA/Industry start UK-only/one-medium/one-year
  and monetise every extension).
- The **risk is not that the licence is too broad to be valid**, but that, having removed the model's usual
  protections-by-expiry (rights lapse; re-clearing pays them again), the draft is currently **thin on the
  replacement protections** a model's adviser will expect. The **six fixes in §5 (esp. AI carve-out,
  revoke-on-breach/takedown, portfolio+credit, and a consideration recital)** close most of that gap cheaply
  without touching the disruption thesis.
- Recommend the solicitor review add three explicit questions to the draft's existing list: **(a) AI/synthetic-
  likeness**, **(b) revocability-on-breach + takedown**, and **(c) consumer-fairness of a perpetual single-fee
  buyout where the model may be a consumer/sole trader** (links to the draft's own cl. 10/consumer-law point).
